How extra mortgage payments save years and thousands

15 October 2026 · 1 min read · Jump to the calculator

Most of us see a mortgage as a fixed deal: a set payment, a set number of years. But the schedule is only a default. Small changes early on can reshape the whole loan.

Why early extra payments are so powerful

A mortgage is front-loaded with interest. In the first years, most of each payment pays the lender, not the loan. That means a dollar you put towards the balance early removes interest not just next month, but for every remaining month of the term.

A worked example

A $250,000 mortgage at 6.5% over 25 years has a normal payment of about $1,688 a month. Over the full term you would pay roughly $256,405 in interest, more than the amount you originally borrowed.

Now add $150 a month:

NormalWith +$150/month
Monthly payment$1,688$1,838
Total interest$256,405$204,113
Paid off in25 years20 years 8 months

That is about $52,292 less interest and 4 years 4 months sooner. The extra cost you $150 a month; it returns about $52,000 over the life of the loan.

Things to check before you start

  • Prepayment rules. Some lenders charge for paying extra or cap it. Ask.
  • Where the money goes. Make sure extra payments reduce the balance, rather than being held for next month’s payment.
  • Your other debts. If you have a card at 22%, clearing that first usually earns you more than extra mortgage payments at 6.5%.
  • Your safety net. Money paid into a mortgage is hard to get back out. Keep an emergency fund first.

Assumptions behind these results

  • Your normal payment is the level payment that clears the mortgage exactly over the years left, at a fixed rate.
  • The extra amount is paid every month on top, and goes straight to the balance (no prepayment penalty).
  • Interest is charged monthly on the outstanding balance (rate ÷ 12).
  • Insurance, rates changes, fees and taxes are not included.
