What minimum credit card payments really cost

16 October 2026 · 2 min read · Jump to the calculator

A minimum payment feels like a safe, responsible number. The bank chose it, so it must be reasonable. But the minimum is designed to keep you paying for as long as possible, because that is how the lender earns.

How the minimum works

Most cards set the minimum at the interest charged that month, plus about 1% of what you owe (with a small floor, such as 25). Two things follow from that:

  1. Almost all of your payment goes to interest at the start.
  2. As the balance shrinks, so does the minimum, so the payments get smaller just as you are making progress.

That second point is the trap. The payment keeps falling, the balance keeps falling slowly, and the debt stretches on.

An example with real numbers

Take an $8,000 balance at 22% APR, with no new spending.

PlanTime to pay offTotal interest
Minimums only (first payment about $227)23 years 1 month$13,600
Minimums + $50 a month7 years 9 months$5,937
Minimums + $100 a month4 years 10 months$3,941
Minimums + $200 a month2 years 10 months$2,391

An extra $100 a month, about the price of a few takeaways, saves roughly $9,659 in interest and gets you out of debt more than 18 years sooner. The reason is simple: every extra dollar goes straight at the balance, so next month’s interest is charged on less, and the effect compounds in your favour instead of the bank’s.

Why small extras work so well

Interest at 22% is about 1.8% a month. Paying $100 extra off an $8,000 balance in month one reduces every future month’s interest, and that saving is reinvested into the balance automatically. You are earning a guaranteed 22% on every extra dollar, which is hard to beat anywhere else.

What to do with this

  • Find your real APR and balance on your statement.
  • Decide on an extra amount you can keep up, even if it is small.
  • Set it up as an automatic payment so it happens without willpower.

Assumptions behind these results
