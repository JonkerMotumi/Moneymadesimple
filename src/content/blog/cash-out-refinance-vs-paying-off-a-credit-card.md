It sounds smart: use your home’s equity to wipe out a 22% credit card and swap it for a mortgage rate in single digits. A lower rate, one payment, and the card is gone. But "cheaper per month" and "cheaper overall" are different things.

## Two things change at once

A cash-out refinance changes the **rate** on the card debt, which helps. It also changes the **rate on your whole mortgage** and **stretches the repayment**, which can hurt a lot. You are not just refinancing the card, you are refinancing everything.

## An example

Say you have a $12,000 card at 22% that you pay $400 a month towards, and a $200,000 mortgage at 4% with 25 years left. Rates have risen, and a new 25-year mortgage would be 6.5% with $3,000 in fees rolled into the loan.

| | Keep both | One new mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly payments | $1,456 | $1,452 |
| Card cleared in | 3 years 8 months | n/a (inside the mortgage) |
| Total cost of borrowing | $122,283 | $223,509 |

The monthly payment barely moves, and the refinance costs about **$101,225** more in total. The reason: you swapped a cheap 4% mortgage for a 6.5% one on the *whole* $200,000, just to clear a $12,000 card you were already paying off in under four years.

## When it can win

If your current mortgage rate is no higher than the new one, and the card would otherwise take a long time to clear, rolling it in can save real money. With a slower card payment of $250 a month, a new rate of 4% and no fees, the same example *saves* about **$10,175**, and lowers the monthly payment by around $187.

## Three questions to ask

1. What is the new rate on my **entire** mortgage, not just the card portion?
2. How long will I take to repay the card if I keep it, and can I pay it faster?
3. If I refinance, will I keep making the old payment so the debt really does clear sooner?
