Almost everyone puts off saving at some point. Money is tight, there is always something more urgent, and five years doesn’t sound like much. The numbers say otherwise.

## The same habit, a different start date

Imagine saving $300 a month and earning an assumed 7% a year, from age 30 to 65.

| | Start at 30 | Start at 35 |
|---|---|---|
| You put in | $126,000 | $108,000 |
| You end with | $540,316 | $365,991 |

By waiting five years you put in only **$18,000 less**, but you finish with **$174,325 less**. The missing five years cost nearly ten times the money you didn’t save.

## Why

Growth on growth. Early deposits don’t just earn returns, they earn returns on their returns for decades. The first five years of contributions do the heaviest lifting because they have the longest to compound. Miss them and you can never make up the time, only the money.

## Catching up costs more than you think

If you start at 35 and want to reach the same $540,000 at 65, you would need to save about **$443 a month** instead of $300. That is nearly 50% more, every month, for the rest of your career. Waiting ten years makes it about $667.

## Important honesty

The 7% in this example is an assumption, not a promise. Real returns bounce around, fees take a share, and inflation reduces what a dollar buys. The *shape* of the result, that time in the market beats almost everything, holds up. The exact figures will not.

## What to do

You don’t need a perfect plan to begin. Start with an amount that feels almost too small, automate it, and raise it whenever you get a pay rise. A modest start today usually beats a big start later.
